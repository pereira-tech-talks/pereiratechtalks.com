# Governance

This document describes how Pereira Tech Talks (PTT) is governed: who makes decisions, how disagreements are resolved, how money flows, and how leadership rotates.

## Mission & Scope

Pereira Tech Talks exists to:

- Build a sustainable technical community in Pereira (Risaralda, Colombia) and project it internationally
- Run free, accessible, high-quality monthly meetups
- Run the annual Pereira Tech Day conference
- Operate vertical programs: Speaker School, La Biblioteca del Mañana, AI / Agents Channel
- Maintain `pereiratechtalks.org` as a public, bilingual community website

PTT is **not** a for-profit company. We have no equity, no employees, no products to sell. We exist as a community; sponsors fund operations.

## Roles

### 1. Community Members

Anyone who attends a meetup, joins a channel, or watches our content is a community member. No formal application required.

### 2. Contributors

Anyone who has contributed at least once in any form (talk, blog post, code PR, mentorship, volunteer hour, sponsorship). Contributors are recognized in the public [Contributors directory](https://pereiratechtalks.org/contributors).

### 3. Vertical Leads

Each vertical (Speaker School, La Biblioteca del Mañana, AI Channel, Monthly Meetups) has one or two leads responsible for:

- Programming the calendar of that vertical
- Recruiting speakers / mentors / curators for that vertical
- Owning content quality (recap posts, slides, recordings)
- Reporting status to the organizers' meeting

Vertical leads are appointed by consensus among current organizers. Term: **12 months**, renewable.

### 4. Organizers

The organizers are the **operational core**. They:

- Run logistics for meetups, events, and Pereira Tech Days
- Manage sponsors, finances, and venue relationships
- Make day-to-day decisions
- Vote on strategic decisions (see Decision-Making below)

Organizers are appointed by consensus among current organizers, typically after a sustained pattern of contribution (≥ 6 months as a vertical lead, mentor, or active contributor). Term: **12 months**, renewable.

The organizer team commits to a **maximum of 7 active members** to keep meetings practical.

### 5. Conduct Team

A 3-person sub-team responsible for receiving and reviewing Code of Conduct reports. Members are appointed by organizers and are expected to:

- Respond to reports within 72 hours
- Maintain confidentiality
- Recuse themselves when there is a conflict of interest

The Conduct Team is empowered to apply the enforcement guidelines in the [Code of Conduct](./CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) without further organizer approval, except for permanent bans (which require organizer consensus).

### 6. Mentors

Speaker School mentors and ad-hoc mentors. Listed publicly with their consent.

## Decision-Making

| Decision Type | Who decides | How |
|---|---|---|
| Day-to-day logistics (venue, catering, speakers' confirmation) | Organizers | Lazy consensus on Discord / WhatsApp |
| Vertical programming | Vertical lead + the relevant organizer | Vertical lead has final say |
| New vertical | All organizers | Consensus, optionally tabled to a written proposal |
| New organizer | All current organizers | Consensus |
| Sponsorship contracts | Sponsorship-lead organizer + 1 other | Sign-off |
| Code of Conduct enforcement (warning, temp ban) | Conduct Team | Majority within team (2 of 3) |
| Code of Conduct enforcement (permanent ban) | Organizers | Consensus |
| Major budget allocation (> 5M COP per single line) | All organizers | Consensus |
| Brand changes | All organizers + brand lead | Consensus + written brief |
| Domain / legal entity / partnership changes | All organizers | Consensus + 1-week notice |

### Lazy consensus

For most decisions, an organizer proposes an action. If no other organizer objects within 48 hours, the action is approved. Objections must include a reason and a counter-proposal.

### Consensus

For higher-stakes decisions, all organizers must explicitly say "yes" or "stand-aside". A "stand-aside" means "I don't agree, but I won't block — proceed". A single "block" stops the decision; the proposer must address the blocker's concerns and re-propose.

### Tie-breaker

If consensus cannot be reached after two rounds, the **founding organizer** (currently Sergio Florez) has a tie-breaker vote, used sparingly and explained publicly.

## Conflict Resolution

Conflicts between community members:

1. Direct conversation between the parties (encouraged but not required).
2. Mediation by an organizer or a Conduct Team member if requested.
3. Code of Conduct report if the conflict involves harassment or violation.

Conflicts between organizers:

1. Direct conversation.
2. Mediation by the founding organizer or an external mediator agreed upon by both parties.
3. If unresolved, the matter is brought to the next organizers' meeting for collective discussion.

## Financial Transparency

PTT operates on a **public ledger basis**:

- Annual budget is published at <https://pereiratechtalks.org/sponsors#budget> at the start of each fiscal year (January).
- A summary of inflows (sponsorships, donations) and outflows (venues, swag, food, recording, travel) is published quarterly.
- Sponsors who request anonymity are listed by tier without name; named sponsors are listed publicly.
- All bank accounts are jointly held by at least two organizers.
- Any organizer can request a full ledger audit at any time; the request triggers a 14-day window to deliver receipts.

PTT operates a small contingency fund (target: 6 months of operating costs) for emergencies (venue cancellation, equipment loss, etc.). Use of the contingency requires consensus.

## Membership of Organizers — Onboarding & Offboarding

### Onboarding

A candidate is proposed at the monthly organizers' meeting. The proposing organizer presents:

- The candidate's contribution history (link to their entry in the Contributors directory)
- The role they're stepping into
- Any specific responsibilities they're taking over

The candidate has a 1-month "trial" attending organizers' meetings as an observer. After that, organizers vote (consensus). On approval, the candidate is added to all relevant channels and signers.

### Offboarding

Organizers can step down at any time with 4 weeks' notice (longer if responsible for an active workstream). Offboarding includes:

- Handover of any documents, accounts, and active threads
- Removal from joint accounts and signers
- Update to the Contributors directory marking the role transition
- Optional retirement post on the blog if the organizer wishes

### Inactivity

An organizer who does not attend any organizers' meeting for **3 consecutive months** without prior notice is moved to "alumni" status by consensus of the remaining organizers, after a private check-in. They retain Contributor status and can return as organizer through normal onboarding.

## Trademark & Brand

The names "Pereira Tech Talks", "Pereira Tech Day", "PTT", "PTD", "La Biblioteca del Mañana", and "Speaker School" are reserved by the community. Use of these names by external parties (other meetups, conferences, podcasts) requires written permission from the organizers.

The official PTT logo is the only authorized brand mark. Per-edition Pereira Tech Day kits are layered on top of the umbrella PTT brand. See [docs/BRAND_GUIDE.md](./BRAND_GUIDE.md) and the dev-only Brand Book at `/internal/brand`.

## Retirement Criteria

If PTT ceases operations, the remaining organizers commit to:

1. Honor any active sponsorship commitments to the agreed-upon end date.
2. Publish a final retrospective post on the blog.
3. Archive the website in read-only mode (or hand over to a successor community).
4. Distribute any remaining contingency funds to a Colombian tech-education non-profit, by consensus.

PTT exists for the community, not for itself. If the community no longer needs PTT, PTT steps aside.

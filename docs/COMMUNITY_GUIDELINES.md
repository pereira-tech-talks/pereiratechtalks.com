# Community Guidelines

These guidelines describe **how we behave in PTT spaces**. They complement the formal [Code of Conduct](./CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) (which is the enforceable contract) by adding cultural expectations specific to a learning community.

> **TL;DR** — Be the community member you'd want a first-time attendee to meet on day one.

## In meetups & events (in person)

- **Arrive on time.** Talks start when announced. Late entrance disrupts the speaker.
- **Phone on silent.** Photos are welcome; ringtones are not.
- **Sit toward the front.** It signals engagement to speakers — first-time speakers especially.
- **Ask questions.** "There are no stupid questions" is a real rule here. If you don't understand something, three other people in the room don't either. Ask.
- **Question hygiene:** ask **one** question, keep it under 30 seconds, leave room for the speaker to actually answer.
- **Don't monopolize speakers.** If you want a deeper conversation, exchange contacts and continue offline.
- **Help newcomers.** If you see someone alone after a talk, introduce yourself.
- **Pay it forward.** Bring a friend, especially someone outside your usual professional circle.

## In meetups & events (online)

- **Camera optional, microphone considerate.** Mute when not speaking. Camera is encouraged during Q&A but never required.
- **Use the chat for questions.** Moderators triage them in real time.
- **No chat noise during the talk.** Keep banter for the after-party room.
- **Don't share recordings without permission.** Recordings are uploaded to YouTube within 7 days when speakers consent.

## In channels (Discord, WhatsApp, Telegram)

- **Use the right channel.** `#general` for casual, `#help` for technical questions, `#meetups` for event chatter, vertical-specific channels for vertical content.
- **Search before asking.** Most questions have been asked before. Search first; if you can't find the answer, ask — it's still better than not asking.
- **One question, one thread.** Use threads for follow-ups so the channel stays scannable.
- **No DMs without consent.** If you want to message someone privately, ask in the public channel first ("can I DM you about X?").
- **No recruiting in `#general`.** Use the dedicated `#jobs` channel and follow its format (one post per company per week, all info in one message — no "DM me for details" gatekeeping).
- **Code-of-conduct violations are not "drama".** Report them via <conduct@pereiratechtalks.org>; don't escalate in public.

## On social media (X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube)

- **Tag generously.** When you talk about PTT, tag `@pertechtalks` so others can find us.
- **Quote responsibly.** When quoting a speaker, attribute clearly and link to the original recording or slides.
- **Disagree well.** Public disagreement with a speaker or organizer is welcome — do it on the substance, not the person.

## Giving feedback

- **In public, praise; in private, critique.** When you publicly praise someone, you make them more likely to keep contributing. When you publicly critique someone, you make first-time contributors afraid.
- **Specific > general.** "I liked your talk" is fine; "I liked how you broke down the trade-offs in section 2" is gold.
- **For speakers, share post-talk feedback within 7 days.** Email or DM. Speakers are doing this for free; warm feedback is the only currency we have.

## Asking for mentorship

- **Be specific.** "Can you mentor me?" is hard to answer. "I want to give my first talk on X by Y date — would you have 2 hours over the next 6 weeks?" is easy.
- **Respect the schedule.** Mentors are volunteering time. Show up on time, prepared.
- **Pay it forward.** If you've been mentored once, plan to mentor someone within 12 months.

## Speaker etiquette

- **Time is sacred.** A 25-minute talk means **25 minutes**, not 30. Practice a buffer.
- **Slides ship in advance.** Drop them in the speakers' channel **48 hours before** the meetup so the AV team can verify them.
- **Be available for Q&A.** Stay 15 minutes after your talk for hallway questions.
- **Recap your own talk.** A blog post or thread within 2 weeks gets your talk the audience it deserves.
- **Decline reasons are valid.** If you don't want to speak on a topic or with a co-speaker, just say so. We'll never push.

## Inclusion

- **Pronouns.** Default to asking, never assume.
- **Spanish + English.** Both languages are first-class. Speakers can present in either; questions can be in either; we'll translate as needed.
- **First-language anxiety.** Many speakers are presenting in their second or third language. Be patient with phrasing; we listen for ideas, not accent.
- **Accessibility.** When a speaker shares a code snippet, they describe it briefly so people who can't see the screen can still follow. AV adds captions to live streams when possible.
- **No "well, actually …" without offering value.** Pedantic correction without learning is exhausting. If you're going to correct, also teach.

## Things that get you talked to

- Persistent interrupting of speakers
- Repeated DMs after a "no, thank you"
- Bringing up someone's identity to dismiss their work ("you wouldn't understand because you're …")
- Treating Q&A as your moment to monologue
- Recruiting outside `#jobs`
- Posting AI-generated content without disclosure (if you co-wrote with an LLM, say so — we love AI; we hate uncredited slop)

## Things that get you reported

- Anything in [§2 of the Code of Conduct](./CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md#2-our-standards) under "unacceptable behavior".

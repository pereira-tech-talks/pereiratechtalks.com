## From the UTP to Academia and Industry: Stories of Professional Growth

On December 12, 2024 we gathered at **Universidad Tecnológica de Pereira** for
**From UTP to academia and business: stories of professional growth**.

Do not miss our last meetup of the year — there will be natilla, buñuelos, and
plenty of surprises.

**TALK 1**

**Speaker:** Sebastian Gomez

**Role:** Software Engineer at Google Zurich

**My career path: from the UTP to Machine Learning Engineer at Google**

If you feel university did not prepare you well for professional life, you are
not alone. In my experience, course quality at our universities depends heavily
on who teaches them, and is generally fairly low. The ones who pay for that are
the students, not the university.

This talk shares tips from my own experience for building real professional
skills. I have had the privilege of working with people from many countries, and
I have seen how our education system puts us at a serious disadvantage next to
people educated elsewhere. I want to share some of what helped me along the way —
programming skills among them — and also what I wish I had done differently, like
contributing more to open source.

It is never too late to start doing things differently, but the earlier you take
your career into your own hands, the better. Do not assume that what you are
taught at university leaves you ready for a successful professional life.

**TALK 2**

**Speaker:** Juan David Gil López

**Role:** Director of Machine Learning at Factored

**Ups, downs, and resilience: my career path in tech**

Have you ever felt that things are not working out professionally in tech? That
you want to apply what you know but the opportunities do not come? That you are
stuck, and have been rejected everywhere? Believe me, you are neither the first
nor the last — a career in tech can have many ups and downs, but with discipline,
resilience, and patience the path gets clearer.

In this talk I want to share some of my career, which started at the UTP almost
11 years ago and has been full of peaks and troughs: a thousand rejections,
months of feeling stuck, and then becoming Director of Machine Learning at a
startup, leading a team of nearly 80 engineers.

Part of the conversation is the tips that kept me afloat in the hardest moments,
and the skills that opened the door to where I am now. The message comes down to
this: there is no single recipe for success, we can each grow differently, and
what matters most is never stopping.

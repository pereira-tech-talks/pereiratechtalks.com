## Lightning-talk meetup

On Wednesday 23 September 2026 we met online, at 6:30 p.m. Colombia time, for a night of lightning talks. On the programme: Camilo Buitrago, Steven Pineda, Sebastian Franco, Alejandro E. Rendón and Juan Sebastian Betancur.

Five ideas, five minutes each: MCP driving a real phone, the coming wave of AI and biotechnology, responsibility in LLMs, networks that still work without the Internet, and a career in tech without a university degree.

### Talks

**MCP for mobile: like Playwright MCP, but in your pocket**

**Speaker:** Camilo Buitrago

How an AI agent can control a real device. Camilo compares this with Playwright MCP, already used in the browser, and shows the same pattern on Android and iOS, with a video demo of the flow running in practice. He covers the problems he hit while setting it up, how ready the technology is today, and what it still lacks.

**The coming wave**

**Speaker:** Steven Pineda

AI, biotechnology, and the future we are building. Based on The Coming Wave by Mustafa Suleyman: will we control the wave, or will the wave control us?

**Is it right for AI to make decisions? Reflections on blame and responsibility in LLMs**

**Speaker:** Sebastian Franco

LLMs can make decisions from prior context and the current task, but those decisions are not wrapped in a sense of responsibility, blame, or even foresight. In five minutes we look at what the decision limits of LLMs should be, and why — even though it is dangerous — they seem to be doing it well.

**What if the Internet goes down tomorrow? Experiments in communicating without infrastructure**

**Speaker:** Alejandro E. Rendón

Today we take for granted that we can communicate at any moment, but almost all of our communication depends on infrastructure we do not control: the Internet, mobile networks, operators and large data centres. What would happen if that infrastructure failed? I have been experimenting with relatively inexpensive hardware — mainly ESP32, Wi-Fi and LoRa — to explore a simple question: can we build small communication networks between people that still work when the Internet is missing or severely limited? The interesting part is not the hardware itself, but the possibilities that appear when we stop assuming every conversation has to go through the Internet.

**I made it without the degree — you can make it with one**

**Speaker:** Juan Sebastian Betancur

The degree is not the real filter. What opens doors in tech is what you can demonstrate, not the diploma you hold. Coming from outside forces you to compensate with a portfolio and real projects. You already have the formal base Juan Sebastian lacked. The message is not "the degree does not matter"; it is "if I closed that gap without the base, you with the base have no reason not to try."
